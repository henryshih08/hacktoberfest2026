How images are selected

High-detail aerial imagery is preferred from your selected year. Otherwise, satellite passes are searched within ±45 days with less than 20% reported scene-wide cloud. NAIP aerial imagery follows a ≤10% cloud product standard; your crop’s cloud cover is not measured.

SOURCES & METHOD

Aerial detail. Satellite history.

The viewer checks USDA’s current NAIP service, USGS The National Map, California’s archive, and the Planetary Computer historical catalogue. It prefers the finest native resolution available for your requested year, including 30 cm imagery in some locations. Coverage varies by state and year. These are aircraft photographs; exact flight dates or date ranges are shown, and a different year is never silently substituted.

USGS describes NAIP tiles as allowing at most 10% cloud cover under the product specification. We disclose this basis; a measured cloud percentage is not available for these tiles. Satellite fallbacks still require reported cloud cover strictly below 20%.

Color matching is on by default. It balances brightness, contrast, and color using both images’ overlapping valid pixels. It changes the display only and can reduce real color differences. Choose Original colors for interpretation of vegetation, water, or surface colors.

Recent imagery: Copernicus Sentinel-2. Historical imagery: USGS Landsat Collection 2 Level-2 is available through Microsoft Planetary Computer from August 22, 1982 onward. Coverage depends on satellite passes, processing, and location. New scenes may arrive after acquisition.

Groundshift searches a ±45-day window around your target dates, only includes scenes with less than 20% scene-wide cloud cover, and lists the nearest passes whose footprints cover your selected area. The two chosen scenes are rendered into the same geographic bounds and pixel grid. No scientific image registration or atmospheric normalization beyond the source product is added.

Recent dates use Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A true-color imagery at 10-meter native resolution, available in this catalogue from June 27, 2015. Older dates use Landsat at 30 meters. If no qualifying Sentinel-2 pass covers the selection, the app explicitly labels its Landsat fallback. Aerial mosaics are rendered at up to 4,000 pixels per side (3,072 for Planetary Computer tiles); satellite images use up to 2,048 pixels. Output size follows native detail rather than adding invented detail. Smaller selections reveal more source detail. Native resolution still limits visible detail, and different sensors can look different. No change detection or annotations are inferred for arbitrary scenes.

The catalogue covers all 50 US states, including Alaska and Hawaii. Each selected map view is limited to 2° per axis and aerial images to a maximum side of 4,000 pixels. The basemap is current OpenStreetMap cartography, not historical imagery. The original reviewed Las Vegas example remains available separately.
